## Overview
Occasional anxiety is a normal part of life, but anxiety disorders involve frequent intense, excessive, and persistent worry and fear about everyday situations. They can include sudden episodes of intense anxiety or terror that peak within minutes, often described as panic attacks. These feelings can interfere with daily activities, be difficult to control, seem out of proportion to actual danger, and may lead people to avoid certain situations.

## Symptoms
Common signs and symptoms include feeling nervous, restless, or tense; sensing danger or doom; increased heart rate; rapid breathing; sweating; trembling; fatigue; trouble concentrating; trouble sleeping; digestive discomfort; difficulty controlling worry; and avoiding triggers. Different anxiety disorders can present in different ways, including social anxiety, panic disorder, generalized anxiety disorder, separation anxiety, and specific phobias.

## When to see a doctor
People should seek professional help if worry is excessive and interferes with work, relationships, or other parts of life, or if anxiety feels upsetting and hard to control. Help is also important when anxiety appears alongside depression, alcohol or drug problems, or concerns about a physical health issue. If someone has suicidal thoughts or behaviours, crisis help should be contacted immediately.

## Causes
The causes of anxiety disorders are not fully understood. Traumatic experiences can trigger anxiety in people who are already prone to it, and inherited traits may also contribute. In some cases, anxiety may be linked to an underlying medical condition or be a side effect of medication.

## Risk factors and prevention
Factors that can increase risk include trauma, stress due to illness, a buildup of stressful events, personality traits, other mental health disorders, family history, and drug or alcohol use or withdrawal. While no one can predict anxiety disorders with certainty, getting help early, staying active, maintaining relationships, and avoiding alcohol or drug use may reduce the impact of symptoms.
