## What is anxiety
Anxiety is often described as a feeling of fear or unease, and it is something many people experience at times. It can be a natural response to pressure, feeling afraid, or feeling threatened, and it may affect how we feel physically, mentally, and behaviourally. Anxiety can become a problem when it feels overwhelming, intense, or persistent enough to interfere with everyday life or relationships.

## Signs or symptoms of anxiety
Anxiety can affect the mind, body, and behaviour. People may feel tearful, get headaches, avoid people or situations, feel tired, restless, irritable, shaky, dizzy, or sweaty, struggle to concentrate or make decisions, have trouble sleeping, worry about the past or future, feel muscle pain or stomach discomfort, or notice shortness of breath and a stronger or irregular heartbeat.

## What causes anxiety
Anxiety can be caused by many different situations and life experiences, and the experience is personal for each person. Sometimes there are no obvious triggers, which can itself feel upsetting or stressful. Possible causes can include upbringing, environment, life events, and temperament.

## Tips on managing anxiety
Helpful self-care steps include mindfulness, meditation, breathing exercises, and relaxation to shift attention back to the present moment. Other useful strategies include structured self-help, keeping a diary to understand triggers, setting aside a daily worry time, and gradually facing feared situations instead of relying on avoidance. Practising these steps regularly can make them more effective.

## More help and support for anxiety
If anxiety is affecting daily life, people are encouraged to contact NHS 111 or speak with a GP, and in England they may be able to self-refer to NHS talking therapies. The page also points people to additional support options, including self-help guides, charities, and other trusted organisations.
