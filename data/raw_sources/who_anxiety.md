## Overview
Everyone can feel anxious sometimes, but anxiety disorders involve fear and worry that are intense, excessive, difficult to control, and disruptive to everyday life. These experiences are often accompanied by physical tension and behavioural or cognitive symptoms. They can last for a long time if untreated and may affect family life, social life, school, and work.

## Symptoms and patterns
People with an anxiety disorder may experience excessive fear or worry about a specific situation, such as panic or social situations, or about many everyday situations. Symptoms often continue for several months and may lead people to avoid what makes them anxious. Other symptoms can include trouble concentrating, feeling tense or restless, nausea or abdominal discomfort, heart palpitations, sweating, trembling, trouble sleeping, and a sense of impending danger or doom.

## Contributing factors and prevention
Anxiety disorders result from a complex interaction of social, psychological, and biological factors. Anyone can experience them, but people who have gone through abuse, major losses, or other adverse experiences may be at higher risk. Community-based prevention can include parental education, school programmes that strengthen social and emotional learning, and exercise programmes for adults.

## Diagnosis and treatment
There are several effective treatments for anxiety disorders, and people with symptoms should seek care. Psychological interventions, especially approaches based on cognitive behavioural therapy, are essential treatments and can be delivered in person, online, individually, or in groups. Stress management skills, relaxation, and mindfulness can also help reduce symptoms. Antidepressants such as SSRIs can be useful for some adults, while benzodiazepines are generally not recommended because of dependence risk and limited long-term effectiveness.

## Self-care
Self-care can support treatment and overall well-being. Helpful actions include reducing or avoiding alcohol, not using illicit drugs, exercising regularly, keeping regular eating and sleeping habits, learning relaxation techniques such as slow breathing or progressive muscle relaxation, and developing mindfulness meditation habits.
